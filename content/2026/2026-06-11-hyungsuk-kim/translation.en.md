# No One Has All the Pieces

No one has all the pieces.

When one part is strong, the opposite attribute is often weak. Among people who have something they want to make, many lack the ability to make it themselves. Conversely, among people who can make almost anything, many do not know exactly what they should make. It may seem ideal for a person who has something they want to make to also be good at making it, but that often takes a long time and the quality can be discouraging.

The power of collaboration, or division of labor, is strong. Pairing a person who wants to make something with a person who can make it is much more efficient than each trying to replace the other’s area. The problem is that finding “someone to work with” is harder than expected. As in the famous metaphor from John Gray’s psychology book *Men Are from Mars, Women Are from Venus*, developers and non-developers often know very little about each other. Their ways of thinking, communicating, and solving problems differ.

Even if a person who wants to make something and a person who can make it somehow collaborate well and create a product or service, that is not the end. Many people in the startup world want to believe that “a good product sells itself,” but in reality far more products are forgotten amid everyone’s indifference. The world is busy. There are fewer customers than one might think who will inspect every available product and make a rational judgment. The ability to make a good product and the ability to sell that product require very different skills.

Many people feel resistance toward “selling.” This is because experiences of being deceived into buying something, or being forced to buy something they did not want, are very powerful. They want to focus only on making a good product and hope someone else will handle sales. But it is very hard to distinguish people who are good at sales from people who merely say they can do sales, so many startups struggle.

Yet there is an even bigger problem.

People who think they can build first and sell later are very likely to build a product that nobody needs. Products made in a meeting room because the team thinks certain features would be nice are difficult to sell even if the world’s greatest salesperson arrives. It is far better to bring in someone who can sell from the moment a product is decided, rather than after it has already been built. That person can tell the makers whether customers are interested in what they are trying to build, and why they think so.

—

“But I’m not trying to start a company. I just work at a company.”

Many of the same things apply inside companies. To make a project succeed, you need people who have at least three elements:

- someone who has something they want to make
- someone who can make it
- someone who can sell what was made, and if customers respond poorly, interpret the reasons and bring that feedback back inside

If one person has all three elements, that is ideal, but in practice this is rare. It is necessary to take interest in and look into what others do, but if you try doing it yourself, you quickly realize it is harder than expected. If circumstances force you to do it, you should not sit idle. You should find a way and do it yourself. But you also need to keep looking for who has the pieces you lack, and carry the intention to persuade them into your project.

In that sense, it is extremely important to observe colleagues “in ordinary times.”

What strengths and weaknesses does each colleague have? Do they keep their word, or do they quietly disappear at some point? Can they explain areas where their thinking differs? How do they resolve differences? What were the results of their past projects? Why did things go well, or why did they not? What do they like and dislike? Is there something they find difficult that I can somehow do? What kind of temperament do they have? Do they speak honestly, or cautiously?

Observe their words, writing, and actions carefully, and think about them. Form an image of what kind of person they are. Then, when the next project comes, define the elements needed to make that project succeed and go looking for the people who have them.

It begins with recognizing people who have different pieces.

# English Summary

The post argues that no one has all the pieces required to make a project succeed. People with strong ideas often lack the ability to build them, while people who can build almost anything may not know what should be built. Collaboration is more efficient than trying to replace each other’s strengths.

The author then adds a third missing piece: sales and customer interpretation. A good product rarely sells itself. If a team builds first and only later tries to find someone who can sell, it may discover that it has made something nobody needs. Someone who understands customer interest and can bring that feedback back into the team should join from the moment the product is decided.

The same applies inside companies. Successful projects need people who want to make something, people who can make it, and people who can sell or interpret customer reactions. Therefore, it matters to observe colleagues in ordinary times: their strengths, weaknesses, reliability, communication style, conflict handling, and past project outcomes. Project success begins with recognizing who has the different pieces.

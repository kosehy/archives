# Figure Notes - The impact of China’s industrial rise on the euro area

## figure-01 - Chart A

- File: `figures/figure-01.png`
- Source: ECB Chart A, Trade Data Monitor and ECB staff calculations.
- Shows: China’s shares of euro area imports by technological content and use, plus average annual growth comparisons across periods.
- Why it matters: Establishes that China’s euro-area import presence has grown and shifted toward medium/high-tech and intermediate-product categories.
- Evidence type: Data chart based on trade data and ECB calculations.
- Caveat: Import shares are value terms for goods only; latest observations November 2025.

## figure-02 - Chart B

- File: `figures/figure-02.png`
- Source: ECB Chart B, Trade Data Monitor and ECB staff calculations.
- Shows: Sectoral exposure to China, separating exposure to China’s intermediate products from exposure to China’s final products.
- Why it matters: Explains why Chinese import penetration affects sectors differently: some mainly face final-good competition, while others also receive cheaper intermediate inputs.
- Evidence type: Data/scatter chart from sectoral import-share changes.
- Caveat: Exposures are yearly average changes in China’s import shares by sector for 2001-22.

## figure-03 - Chart C

- File: `figures/figure-03.png`
- Source: ECB Chart C, Trade Data Monitor, TiVA 2025 release and ECB staff calculations.
- Shows: Estimated production-growth effect of exposure to Chinese imports: intermediate-goods exposure is positive, final-goods exposure is negative.
- Why it matters: Quantifies the article’s core asymmetry between input cost channel and competition channel.
- Evidence type: Econometric result chart.
- Caveat: The result depends on the country-sector panel, Bartik-style instrument, fixed effects and sector trend specification.

## figure-04 - Chart D

- Files: `figures/figure-04a.png`, `figures/figure-04b.png`
- Source: ECB Chart D, ECB staff DSGE model based on Gnocato et al. (2025).
- Shows: Panel a decomposes EU imports from China by sector/use; panel b simulates EU sectoral production deviations after persistent Chinese productivity shocks.
- Why it matters: Shows the model mechanism: advanced manufacturing imports are more intermediate-input intensive and can support EU production, while traditional manufacturing shocks can create stronger displacement in exposed EU sectors.
- Evidence type: Model simulation chart.
- Caveat: Counterfactual simulation, not an empirical forecast.

## figure-05 - Chart E

- Files: `figures/figure-05a.png`, `figures/figure-05b.png`
- Source: ECB Chart E, ECB staff DSGE model based on Gnocato et al. (2025).
- Shows: Simulated effects of persistent Chinese productivity shocks on EU GDP and inflation.
- Why it matters: Captures the article’s macro conclusion: cheaper Chinese goods can support short-run EU GDP and exert disinflationary pressure, especially through advanced-manufacturing cost channels.
- Evidence type: Model simulation chart.
- Caveat: The model does not fully quantify longer-run scarring, strategic vulnerabilities or structural dependency risks.

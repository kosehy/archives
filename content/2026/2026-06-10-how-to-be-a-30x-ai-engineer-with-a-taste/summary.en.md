# Summary: How to Be a 30x AI Engineer with a Taste

## One-sentence summary

As AI commoditizes code generation and implementation speed, the differentiating engineering skill becomes `taste`: the quality of a human evaluation function that decides what to build, how to structure it, and when the result is good enough.

## Key points

- The article frames the AI coding era through examples around OpenAI Codex, Claude Code, and OpenClaw.
- `Taste` is not merely aesthetic preference. It is an evaluation capability with three forms.
  - Recognition: seeing which implementation or product decision is better before fully articulating why.
  - Compass: knowing what should be built next, not just evaluating what already exists.
  - Vision: understanding what will matter on a longer trajectory.
- When implementation becomes cheap, value shifts to five zones: problem selection, systems architecture, quality judgment, user empathy, and communication/storytelling.
- Good taste is shown through concrete constraints. Picking Rust or TypeScript is not tasteful by itself. It becomes tasteful when the choice shapes team behavior, model fit, dependency surface, or long-term maintainability.
- The article proposes deliberate practice: study admired tools and papers, compare pairs of designs, write down maker decisions, redesign a real workflow, launch a constrained product, and encode taste into team artifacts such as AGENTS.md.

## Reusable framing

- In the AI era, the differentiator is not output volume but the quality of the evaluation function.
- Taste is a trainable operating system for judgment, not a mysterious personality trait.
- Running more agents matters less than knowing which problem deserves agents at all.
- Once AI takes over typing, the thinking that used to be hidden inside code becomes the visible job.

## Caveats

- The `30x` framing is rhetorical. It should not be reused as a measured productivity multiplier.
- Many examples come from elite AI-tooling environments and may not transfer directly to ordinary organizations.
- The most durable contribution is the judgment-centered frame, not the exact names, dates, or ratios cited in the essay.

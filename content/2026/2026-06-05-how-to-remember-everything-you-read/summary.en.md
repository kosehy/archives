# Summary - How to Remember Everything You Read

## Core thesis
Reading retention is not mainly a matter of willpower or raw memory. It is a workflow problem: preview the structure, read actively, encode ideas in your own words, review them at the right intervals, connect them to existing models, and store only the useful residue in a system you can keep using.

## Main ideas

- **Comprehension comes before retention.** Survey the structure, set questions, read in layers, and delay judgment until you can explain the author's argument fairly.
- **Active reading beats passive exposure.** Marginal notes, sparse highlights, paraphrasing, and self-explanation force the reader to process meaning rather than merely recognize words.
- **Memory needs retrieval, not just rereading.** Spaced repetition, active recall, immediate 24-hour review, and interleaving are the main retention mechanisms.
- **Synthesis turns reading into knowledge.** Connect ideas to existing mental models, teach them with the Feynman technique, compress them through progressive summarization, and create atomic linked notes.
- **Reading effort should be allocated strategically.** Skim broadly, read selectively, reread only the highest-value material, and move on from low-yield sources.
- **Tools should reduce friction.** Analog notes, PKM apps, spaced repetition software, tags, and quick capture systems help only if they remain simple enough to maintain.
- **Mindset and body matter.** Curiosity, focus, reflection, patience, sleep, exercise, and environment design all affect whether reading becomes durable memory.

## Practical operating rule
Use a four-step loop: **preview → read actively → retrieve/review → synthesize into output**. The output can be a note, card, diagram, question, essay, decision, or teaching explanation.
